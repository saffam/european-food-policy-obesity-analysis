# Policy and Obesity Analysis — Executive Summary

## Study Overview

This exploratory analysis examines the relationship between
country-level obesity prevalence and two policy indicators:

- Policy Count
- Instrument Diversity

The analysis includes 27 country-level observations.

## Statistical Approach

An ordinary least squares (OLS) regression was estimated:

Obesity Prevalence ~ Policy Count + Instrument Diversity

The analysis was complemented by Pearson and Spearman correlation
analysis and regression diagnostic checks.

## Main Results

The model produced an R-squared of 0.197 and an
adjusted R-squared of 0.130.

The overall model F-test had a p-value of 0.072.

The estimated coefficient for Policy Count was:

0.3618

with a p-value of:

0.2340

The estimated coefficient for Instrument Diversity was:

-1.3348

with a p-value of:

0.0387

## Interpretation

The results describe cross-country statistical associations within
the analysed dataset.

The analysis does not establish that policy count or instrument
diversity causes changes in obesity prevalence.

Potential differences between countries may reflect socioeconomic,
demographic, healthcare-system, environmental and other factors
not included in the model.

## Limitations

- Small cross-country sample.
- Cross-sectional rather than longitudinal design.
- Potential omitted-variable bias.
- Policy indicators are descriptive measures.
- Obesity prevalence estimates may differ in definition,
  measurement and reference period between countries.

## Conclusion

The analysis provides an exploratory quantitative assessment of the
relationship between policy indicators and obesity prevalence and
can be used as a starting point for more detailed comparative
health-policy research.
